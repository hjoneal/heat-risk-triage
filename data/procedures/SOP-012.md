# Oil level and leak inspection

Insulating oil provides both dielectric strength and the heat transfer path from the winding to the radiator. A low level or an active leak reduces both, and the effect is most pronounced under sustained load at high ambient temperature.

## Level check

Read the conservator sight glass with the unit at settled temperature and record the reading against the temperature-compensated mark. A reading below the minimum mark at ambient requires make-up oil before the unit is exposed to a forecast heat event. Do not read the level immediately after a load change; allow the unit to settle.

Where the sight glass is discoloured or the level is not clearly readable, raise a defect for glass replacement rather than recording an estimated level.

## Leak inspection

Inspect the following in order: main tank welds, radiator header gaskets, radiator drain points, bushing turret gaskets, valve glands, and the cooler pipework flanges. Record the location, whether the leak is weeping, seeping or running, and whether staining is present on the plinth or bund floor.

A light film on the bund floor is consistent with a slow leak that may not be visible at the source. Where a film is present and no source is found, mark the unit for re-inspection within 30 days rather than closing the finding.

## Classification

Weeping and seepage are recorded as condition defects and scheduled. A running leak is treated as an immediate defect and escalated the same day. Any leak that has reached the bund is reportable under environmental compliance; see REG-043.

## Make-up oil

Use only oil of the same specification as the existing charge. Record the volume added, the batch reference and the resulting level. A repeated need for make-up oil on the same unit indicates an unfound leak and warrants investigation rather than further topping up.
