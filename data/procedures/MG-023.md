# Insulation ageing and end-of-life indicators

The cellulose insulation in an oil-immersed transformer ages irreversibly with temperature. The rate approximately doubles for every 6 to 8 degree rise in hot-spot temperature, which is why sustained thermal exposure matters more than a brief excursion.

## Ageing mechanism

Ageing proceeds by hydrolysis, oxidation and pyrolysis of the paper insulation, reducing its mechanical strength. A unit with degraded insulation continues to operate normally under steady conditions and fails under mechanical stress: a through-fault, a switching transient, or the thermal cycling of a multi-day heat event.

Ageing is cumulative and cannot be reversed by reconditioning the oil. Oil treatment restores dielectric strength; it does not restore the paper.

## Indicators

The principal indicators available without an outage are:

- Furan concentration in the oil, which correlates with the degree of polymerisation of the paper
- Carbon monoxide and carbon dioxide trends in dissolved gas results
- Cumulative loading history and the hot-spot temperature the unit has experienced
- Age in service, as a proxy where no historical data exists

No single indicator is conclusive. Furan results are interpreted against the unit's own history and its oil replacement record, since oil changes remove the accumulated furan.

## Ageing units under heat exposure

A unit of advanced age with a history of high loading has a reduced margin against mechanical failure and should be prioritised for pre-event attention ahead of a younger unit in otherwise similar condition. The condition that matters is not visible at inspection, which is why age in service is carried as an explicit factor.

Where an ageing unit cannot be attended before an event, apply the loading restriction in MG-021 in preference to accepting the exposure.
