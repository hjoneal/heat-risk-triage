# Transformer overtemperature alarm response

This procedure covers the response to a top oil or winding temperature alarm from a substation transformer.

## Immediate actions

On receipt of an alarm, confirm from the operations desk the present loading, the trend over the preceding six hours, and the ambient temperature at the site. A rising trend with the cooling proved running is a different situation from a step change following a load transfer.

Confirm whether the cooling stages are called and whether they are confirmed running. A common cause of an alarm on an otherwise healthy unit is a cooling system that has been left in manual or has lost its control supply.

## Assessment

Where the cooling is confirmed running and the trend is rising, reduce load. Apply ERP-032 for transfer options. Where transfer is unavailable, disconnection of non-essential load is authorised by the duty operations manager.

Where the cooling is not running, attend site. A unit alarming with the cooling failed during a heat event is at immediate risk and takes priority over planned pre-event work elsewhere.

## Site attendance

Confirm the alarm against the local indicator; do not rely on the telemetered value alone. Check the fan bank, the control supply and the thermostat. Check the radiator surfaces for obvious blockage and the compound for obstruction to airflow.

Do not open the tank or disturb the oil system on an alarming unit.

## Trip

At the trip setting the unit disconnects automatically. Do not re-energise a unit that has tripped on temperature until it has cooled to normal operating temperature and the cause has been established. Re-energising into the same condition will repeat the trip and adds a switching transient to a thermally stressed unit.

Record the event, the peak temperature reached and the duration above alarm for the ageing assessment under MG-023.
