# Bushing and gasket service guidance

Bushings and gasketed joints are the most common source of oil loss and a recurring cause of in-service failure on ageing plant.

## Gasket condition

Nitrile and cork-nitrile gaskets harden with age and thermal cycling. A joint that has been through many heat events will weep before it leaks, typically at the lower face where the head of oil is greatest. Weeping at a radiator header or turret gasket is a condition defect and is scheduled; it is not an immediate defect unless the loss rate is material.

Do not attempt to cure a weeping joint by further tightening. Over-compressed gaskets extrude and the joint then leaks continuously. Replace the gasket.

## Bushing inspection

Inspect porcelain for chips, cracks and tracking, and the oil level in the sight glass of oil-filled bushings. Record any discoloration of the gasket face or evidence of previous repair.

Capacitance and tan delta measurement, where the bushing is fitted with a test tap, is the definitive condition assessment and is carried out at the routine outage interval.

## Torque and reassembly

Reassemble to the specified torque in the specified sequence, in stages, working diagonally. Record the torque used. A joint reassembled without a recorded torque is to be re-checked at the next visit.

## Before a heat event

Weeping joints are not normally a priority ahead of a forecast heat event compared with cooling and airflow defects, but a joint that has begun to run must be addressed. Thermal expansion during the event will increase the loss rate from any joint already losing oil, and a falling level reduces the cooling margin at the point where it is most needed.
