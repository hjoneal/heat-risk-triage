# Heat event readiness and crew deployment

This playbook covers the 72 hours preceding a forecast heat event affecting one or more districts.

## Trigger

The playbook is initiated when the forecast indicates two or more consecutive days with a maximum above 35 degrees, or three or more consecutive nights with a minimum above 24 degrees. Either condition alone is sufficient; sustained warm nights are the more damaging of the two and are frequently overlooked.

## 72 hours before

Establish the ranked list of assets for pre-event attention. Ranking accounts for forecast exposure, the condition recorded at the last inspection, and the number of customers affected by a loss of the asset. Confirm crew availability and the number of interventions achievable before the event begins.

Issue the work instructions with the specific defect identified for each asset. A crew sent to a site without a named defect will carry out a general inspection and will not necessarily address the condition that drove the ranking.

## 48 hours before

Complete cooling and airflow work under SOP-014, SOP-015 and SOP-011. These are the interventions that materially change the outcome and they are to be completed first.

Confirm loading restrictions with the operations desk for any asset whose defect could not be cleared. Apply MG-021.

## 24 hours before

Confirm mobile plant availability under ERP-035 and standby crew rosters. Verify that alarm routing for overtemperature is correct and that the responsible engineer is identified for each district.

## During the event

Monitor overtemperature alarms under ERP-033. Do not despatch crews to site during the hottest part of the day except in response to an alarm. Record any asset that operated abnormally for review after the event.
