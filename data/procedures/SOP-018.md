# Radiator cleaning and fin maintenance

Radiator fouling develops slowly and reduces heat transfer across the entire bank. It is one of the few condition defects that can be corrected cheaply, quickly and immediately before a forecast heat event.

## Assessment

Assess fouling as the proportion of the fin surface obscured by dust, pollen, seed, insect debris or scale. Assess from both faces of the bank; the downwind face fouls faster and is often the only one inspected. Scale build-up on the lower third of a bank is common on coastal and dusty sites.

Cleaning is warranted where more than a quarter of the fin surface is obscured, or where a thermographic survey under SOP-017 shows an elevated fin surface temperature across the bank.

## Method

Isolate and de-energise the unit where the cleaning method requires access within the safety clearance. Where cleaning is carried out with the unit in service, work only from outside the clearance envelope with approved equipment.

Clean with low-pressure water applied along the fin direction, working from the top of the bank downward. Do not use high-pressure equipment: it bends fins closed and permanently reduces the free area. Do not use solvents on painted fin surfaces.

Straighten bent fins with a fin comb of the correct pitch. Fins bent flat against a neighbour carry no airflow and are equivalent to a blocked section.

## After cleaning

Confirm that drain paths from the bank are clear and that no water has entered the control cabinet or fan motor enclosures. Prove the cooling stages under SOP-014 before returning the unit to normal control.

Record the proportion of the bank cleaned and the assessed condition afterwards, so that the fouling rate for the site can be established over successive visits.
