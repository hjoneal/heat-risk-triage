# Vegetation and compound clearance around substation plant

Airflow to radiator banks and ventilation openings must remain unobstructed at all times. Encroaching vegetation is the most common cause of restricted airflow on rural sites and is a recurring finding at inspection.

## Clearance requirement

Maintain a minimum clearance of two metres between any growth and the radiator face, and one metre between growth and the compound fence line. Ground cover within the compound is to be kept below 150mm. Where a unit is fitted with side-mounted radiators, clearance is measured from the outermost fin.

## Inspection

At each routine visit, walk the full perimeter and record any growth encroaching on the clearance envelope. Photograph the obstruction and note the aspect. Growth on the prevailing downwind side accumulates wind-blown debris and should be treated as a priority even where the clearance envelope is technically met.

Ventilation grilles and intake louvres are to be checked for accumulated leaf litter, seed heads and nesting material. Bird nesting in upper grilles is common between March and July and can restrict airflow substantially without being visible from ground level.

## Stored material

No material may be stored inside the compound where it obstructs a ventilation opening or the access route to the radiator bank. Pallets, drums and cable reels left against the building are to be removed at the time of discovery, not raised as a defect for later attention.

## Action before a forecast heat event

Where a unit is identified for pre-event attention and a vegetation or obstruction defect is outstanding, clearance work takes precedence over other remedial activity. Restricted airflow removes the cooling margin the unit relies on during sustained high ambient conditions. Record the clearance work against the originating defect and close it out at the time of completion.
