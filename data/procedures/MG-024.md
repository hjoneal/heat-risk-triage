# Conservator and oil preservation system guidance

The conservator accommodates the volume change of the oil charge across the operating temperature range and, with the preservation system, limits the ingress of moisture and oxygen.

## Level and volume

Oil volume changes by approximately 0.07 percent per degree. Across a full operating range this is a substantial volume, and the conservator is sized accordingly. The sight glass carries temperature-compensated marks; a level read without reference to the oil temperature is not meaningful.

A level below the minimum mark at ambient temperature indicates a genuine loss of charge, not thermal contraction. Investigate for a leak before adding make-up oil.

## Breather maintenance

Silica gel breathers are to be inspected at every visit and the desiccant replaced when more than two thirds has changed colour. A saturated breather admits moist air to the conservator on every cooling cycle, and moisture in the oil reduces dielectric strength and accelerates paper ageing.

Check that the oil seal cup is filled to the correct level and that the breather is not cracked.

## Rubber bag and membrane systems

Where an air cell or membrane is fitted, confirm that the vent path is clear and that the cell has not ruptured. A ruptured cell is indicated by oil in the breather line and by a level that no longer responds to temperature.

## Before a heat event

Confirm the level, confirm the breather condition, and confirm that the conservator vent path is clear. A unit that cannot accommodate thermal expansion will vent oil during the event, which is both an environmental incident and a loss of charge going into the highest-stress period.
