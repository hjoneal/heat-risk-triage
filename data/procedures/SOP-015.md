# Ventilation louvre and airflow inspection

Indoor and enclosure-mounted units depend on the building ventilation path to shed heat. This procedure covers the inspection of intake and exhaust louvres, dampers and the airflow path around the unit.

## Louvre condition

Inspect all intake and exhaust louvres for blockage by wind-blown debris, leaf litter, nesting material and accumulated dust. Blockage is assessed as a proportion of free area; anything above one quarter of the free area obstructed is recorded as a defect requiring clearance before a forecast heat event.

Check that louvre blades are not bent, corroded or missing, and that insect mesh where fitted is intact and not blinded by dust.

## Dampers

Where motorised or thermostatic dampers are fitted, prove full travel on a thermostat call. A damper that opens partially restricts airflow throughout the event and is a common cause of unexpected overtemperature on an otherwise healthy unit. Stiff linkages are to be lubricated and re-proved, not merely noted.

## Airflow path

Walk the airflow path from intake to exhaust. Confirm that no stored material, temporary screening or subsequently installed equipment obstructs the path. Where a unit has been retrofitted with additional plant in the same enclosure, confirm that the ventilation design still holds.

Recirculation between an exhaust and a nearby intake produces a rising enclosure temperature through a multi-day event even where every louvre is clear. Where recirculation is suspected, record the enclosure temperature at the intake and escalate for assessment.

## Before a forecast heat event

Obstructed ventilation is to be cleared in preference to deferring it. Clearance is low-cost, immediate, and restores the cooling margin the unit relies on across consecutive warm nights when overnight recovery is already reduced.
