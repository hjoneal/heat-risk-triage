# Ice and snow accretion on substation structures

Freezing precipitation places a structural burden on gantries, busbar supports and overhead conductor spans, and can bring down equipment that is otherwise sound.

## Accretion assessment

Radial ice accretion is assessed on the conductor diameter after a freezing event. Accretion above 12mm radial requires structural assessment of the affected span before any attempt is made to clear it. Wet snow adheres more readily than dry snow and produces the heavier accretion for a given depth of precipitation.

Assess gantry steelwork for asymmetric accretion, which imposes torsion that the structure was not designed to carry.

## Clearance

Do not strike or shake an iced conductor to dislodge accretion. The release is uncontrolled, the span jumps, and phase-to-phase clashing has caused faults on previously healthy circuits.

Where accretion must be removed from walkways, gantry access ladders and platforms, clear by hand with non-metallic tools. Do not apply salt to galvanised steelwork or to concrete plinths.

## Frozen drainage

Frozen site drainage causes standing meltwater to accumulate within the compound and inside containment bunds. Standing meltwater reduces the retained volume available in a bund and must be cleared once the thaw begins.

Check that trench covers and cable duct entries have not been lifted by frost heave.

## Anti-condensation heaters

Control cabinets and mechanism boxes are fitted with anti-condensation heaters. Prove each heater circuit at the autumn visit. A failed heater admits condensation, which corrodes terminations and causes intermittent control faults that present months later.

## Records

Record the accretion assessed, the structures affected and any clearance carried out. Structural findings are referred for engineering assessment rather than closed on site.
