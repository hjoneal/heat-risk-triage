# Forced-air cooling system commissioning and fan control

This note covers the commissioning, control settings and periodic proving of forced-air cooling equipment fitted to oil-immersed transformers.

## Control settings

Cooling stages are called on top oil temperature with a fixed differential to prevent hunting. Stage 1 calls at 65 degrees top oil and drops out at 55 degrees. Stage 2, where fitted, calls at 75 degrees and drops out at 65 degrees. These settings are not to be altered in the field to suppress nuisance operation; frequent calling indicates a loading or cooling problem, not a settings problem.

Where a winding temperature indicator is fitted, the stage call is taken from the winding image rather than top oil, with settings advised separately for the specific unit.

## Fan motor maintenance

Fan motors are sealed for life and require no lubrication. Failure modes in service are bearing seizure, capacitor failure on single-phase units, and corrosion of the terminal enclosure.

Replace a seized fan rather than attempting to free it. A fan that has seized once and been freed will seize again, usually during the event it was needed for.

## Contactor and control supply

A chattering contactor indicates a marginal control supply or a failing coil. It causes the stage to cycle rapidly, which shortens fan motor life and delivers a fraction of the intended cooling. Replace the contactor; do not adjust the thermostat to work around it.

Prove the control supply at the cabinet under load, not at the source.

## Proving

Prove every stage on manual test at each routine visit and before any forecast heat event. Record the number of fans proved against the number fitted. A partially proved bank is a de-rated unit; see MG-021.
