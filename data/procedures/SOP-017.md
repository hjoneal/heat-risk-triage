# Thermographic survey of transformer plant

Infrared survey identifies developing connection faults, blocked radiator sections and failed cooling stages that are not apparent on a visual inspection.

## Survey conditions

Survey under load, ideally at or above 60 percent of rating, with the unit at settled temperature. A survey taken on a lightly loaded unit will not reveal a resistive connection fault. Record the load at the time of survey; results are not comparable across different loading conditions.

Avoid surveying in direct sun on reflective surfaces, and record ambient temperature and wind speed, both of which affect apparent surface temperature.

## What to survey

Cover, in order: HV and LV bushing terminals and connections, the tank surface, each radiator section individually, the cooling fan motors, the cable box or termination enclosure, and the tap changer compartment where accessible.

A radiator section reading materially cooler than its neighbours indicates restricted flow through that section, commonly a partially closed valve or an internal blockage. A section reading hotter than its neighbours across the whole bank indicates fouling of the external fin surface.

## Interpretation

Assess connection findings by the temperature rise above a comparable reference point on the same phase, not by absolute temperature. A rise of 15 degrees or more above reference is actioned before the next heat event; a rise above 40 degrees is treated as immediate.

## Reporting

Record every finding with an image, the reference point used, the load at survey and the ambient conditions. Findings feed the defect register under SOP-016.

Where a survey identifies restricted radiator flow or fouling on a unit that is subsequently identified for pre-event attention, the thermographic evidence should accompany the work instruction so that the crew knows which section to address.
