# Emergency load transfer and feeder switching

Load transfer reduces the loading on a transformer at risk by moving demand to an adjacent asset with available capacity.

## When to transfer

Transfer is considered where a unit is approaching its temperature limit, where a cooling defect cannot be cleared before an event, or where an overtemperature alarm has been received and the load cannot otherwise be reduced.

Transfer is not a substitute for correcting a defect. It moves thermal stress to another asset, and during a heat event the receiving asset is also under elevated ambient exposure.

## Assessment before switching

Confirm the receiving asset's available capacity at the prevailing ambient temperature, applying the de-rating in MG-021 to both assets. A transfer assessed against nameplate rating during a heat event will overload the receiving unit.

Confirm the condition of the receiving asset. Transferring load onto a unit with a known cooling or oil defect is not an improvement.

Confirm the switching sequence, the affected customer count and the interruption duration if the transfer cannot be made on-load.

## Execution

Execute to the approved switching schedule with the operations desk in attendance. Record the time of each operation, the load transferred and the resulting loading on both assets.

## After transfer

Monitor top oil temperature on the receiving asset for the following six hours. Because of the thermal lag, the effect of an increase in load is not fully evident for several hours after the switching operation.

Return the load to its normal configuration only when the original defect has been corrected or the event has passed and both assets have returned to normal operating temperature.
