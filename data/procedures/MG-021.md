# Loading and de-rating at high ambient temperature

Nameplate rating assumes a reference ambient temperature and a full complement of working cooling. Where either condition is not met, the permissible load must be reduced.

## Ambient de-rating

The nameplate rating assumes an average ambient of 30 degrees and a maximum of 40 degrees. Above the reference average, reduce permissible load by 1.5 percent of rating for each degree of average ambient above 30 degrees, assessed over the 24 hours preceding the loading decision.

Assessment on the daily average rather than the daily peak is deliberate. A unit exposed to sustained high ambient with limited overnight recovery is under greater cumulative thermal stress than one that reaches a higher afternoon peak but cools fully overnight.

## De-rating for reduced cooling

Where forced cooling is partially unavailable, reduce permissible load in proportion to the cooling capacity lost:

- One fan failed in a bank of three: reduce by 8 percent of rating
- One full stage unavailable on a two-stage installation: reduce to the ONAN rating
- Cooling control in manual or unproven: treat as one stage unavailable

De-rating for reduced cooling is applied in addition to ambient de-rating, not instead of it.

## Sustained events

On events extending beyond 48 hours with overnight minima above 24 degrees, apply a further 5 percent reduction from the third day onward. Hot-spot temperature under these conditions continues to rise day on day even where the daily peak ambient is unchanged, because the unit does not return to its starting temperature overnight.

## Recording

Record every de-rating applied, the reason, the value and the period. Restore the full rating only when the underlying condition has been corrected and verified, not when the event ends.
